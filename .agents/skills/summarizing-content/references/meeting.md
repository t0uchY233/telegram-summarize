# Meeting, transcript, and notes reference

Read this reference completely before meeting-, transcript-, or notes-specific
work. Use the user's requested language and level of detail. Keep the result
short for a short source; use only sections warranted by the evidence rather
than a fixed minutes template.

## Evidence rules

- Treat the transcript, notes, and supplied artifacts as the record. Attribute
  statements to speakers and timestamps or quotations when available.
- Record a **Decision** only when the source explicitly makes or approves it.
  Keep a proposal, option, suggestion, or tentative agreement separate.
- Record an **Action item** only for an explicit commitment or assignment.
  Name an owner and deadline only when the source states them; otherwise say
  “unassigned” or “deadline not stated,” never infer either.
- Preserve modal language: “can,” “may,” “should,” and “will consider” do not
  establish a completed action or a firm commitment without supporting text.
- Include a **Disagreement** only when competing positions are expressed, and
  identify the speakers and the unresolved state. Include an **Open question**
  only when it is actually raised or left unresolved.
- State rationale, risk, timing, review, and follow-up only when supported. Do
  not falsely attribute a review or meeting to someone merely because they
  mentioned it.

## Flexible concise output

Start with a short **Summary**, then use relevant compact sections such as
**Facts**, **Decisions**, **Proposals**, **Action items**, **Disagreements**,
**Open questions**, **Resources**, **Conclusions**, and **Limitations**. Omit
irrelevant sections instead of filling them with placeholders unless the user
explicitly requests a template. Label missing evidence rather than inventing
meeting metadata, attendance, owners, deadlines, or next meetings.
